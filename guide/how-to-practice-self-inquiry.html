How to Practice Self-Inquiry

The direct path to inner peace taught by Sri Ramana Maharshi.

The core teaching of Tiruvannamalai's most famous sage, Sri Ramana Maharshi, is Self-Inquiry (Atma Vichara). It is a profound meditation technique centered around one simple question: "Who am I?"

The Core Concept

We usually identify ourselves with our body, our thoughts, our job, or our emotions. Self-Inquiry challenges this. If your thoughts are constantly changing, and your body is aging, who is the unchanging witness behind it all?

How to Practice It

  1. Sit Quietly: Find a quiet spot, close your eyes, and let your breathing settle.
  2. Notice the 'I' Thought: Every thought you have starts with 'I' (e.g., "I am tired", "I want this").
  3. Ask the Question: When a thought arises, ask internally, "To whom does this thought arise?" The answer is "To me." Then ask, "Who am I?"
  4. Don't Answer with Words: Do not try to intellectually answer "I am a soul" or "I am awareness." The question is meant to turn your attention backward, away from the thoughts and toward the source of awareness itself.
  5. Rest in the Source: The mind will momentarily go blank or quiet. Rest in that silent gap. When thoughts return, repeat the process.

Connecting with God

In this tradition, God is not a distant figure in the sky, but the very core of your own being. By finding the true "I", you merge with the divine consciousness that is Lord Shiva.
