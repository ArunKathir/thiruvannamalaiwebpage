The Ultimate Guide to Girivalam

Everything you need to know about circumambulating the sacred Arunachala hill.

Girivalam, the sacred circumambulation of the Arunachala hill in Tiruvannamalai, is one of the most revered spiritual practices in Tamil Nadu. The word translates to "Giri" (hill) and "Valam" (coming around). Millions of devotees walk this 14-kilometer path every year, seeking blessings, peace, and self-realization.

Why Perform Girivalam?

According to ancient texts like the Arunachala Puranam, the hill itself is a manifestation of Lord Shiva in the form of fire (Agni Lingam). Walking around the hill barefoot is believed to burn away karma and bring spiritual enlightenment. Sri Ramana Maharshi, the great sage of Tiruvannamalai, highly recommended Girivalam, stating that the hill exudes a magnetic spiritual energy.

The Route and The Ashta Lingams

The total distance of the Girivalam path is approximately 14 kilometers. Along the route, there are eight primary Lingams (Ashta Lingams), each facing a different direction and associated with a different aspect of life:

  • Indra Lingam (East): Represents prosperity and longevity.
  • Agni Lingam (South-East): Represents health and relief from diseases.
  • Yama Lingam (South): Represents the removal of fear and financial prosperity.
  • Niruthi Lingam (South-West): Represents peace of mind.
  • Varuna Lingam (West): Represents social development and health.
  • Vayu Lingam (North-West): Represents the removal of heart diseases and lung ailments.
  • Kubera Lingam (North): Represents wealth and prosperity.
  • Easanya Lingam (North-East): Represents peace and inner realization.

When is the Best Time to Go?

While Girivalam can be performed on any day, it is considered highly auspicious to walk the path during Pournami (Full Moon). However, be prepared for massive crowds—often in the hundreds of thousands.

If you prefer a quieter, more meditative walk, choose any regular weekday. Early mornings (starting at 4:30 AM) or late evenings after sunset are the best times to avoid the intense daytime heat.

Important Rules and Etiquette

  • Walk Barefoot: It is highly recommended to walk without footwear to absorb the energy of the earth and as a sign of humility.
  • Walk on the Left: Always stay on the left side of the road, allowing vehicles and fast walkers to pass on the right.
  • Maintain Silence: Girivalam is a meditative process. Recite "Om Namah Shivaya" or Arunachala chants internally.
  • Dress Modestly: Wear traditional or conservative clothing out of respect for the holy town.

Where to Stay for Girivalam?

If your primary goal is to perform Girivalam, booking a room near the path itself or near Ramana Ashram is ideal. This allows you to start your walk easily early in the morning without needing transport.
