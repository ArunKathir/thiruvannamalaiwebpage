Spiritual Trek

Trekking to Skandasramam & Virupaksha Cave

A journey up the sacred Arunachala Hill to the profound meditation caves of Sri Ramana Maharshi.

The Spiritual Significance of the Caves

For spiritual seekers visiting Tiruvannamalai, a hike up Arunachala Hill to sit in the caves where Sri Ramana Maharshi meditated is a profoundly transformative experience. The mountain itself is considered the physical embodiment of Lord Shiva, and these specific caves hold decades of intense spiritual vibrations.

Virupaksha Cave

Sri Ramana Maharshi stayed in the Virupaksha Cave for 17 years (1899-1916). The cave gets its name from the 13th-century saint Virupaksha Deva, who spent his life here. According to legend, his body turned into ashes upon his death, and these ashes are preserved inside the cave in the form of a Shiva Lingam.

The Vibe: The cave is remarkably silent and has a unique "Om" shape inside. It is maintained by Sri Ramana Ashram and is an extraordinary spot for deep meditation.

Skandasramam

Located slightly higher up the hill from Virupaksha Cave, Skandasramam is where Ramana Maharshi lived from 1916 to 1922. It was built by his devoted disciple Kandaswami (hence the name). This ashram is surrounded by trees and features a beautiful freshwater spring.

The View: From the courtyard of Skandasramam, you get a breathtaking, panoramic view of the majestic Arunachaleswarar Temple below.

The Trekking Route

There are two main paths to reach the caves:

  1. From Sri Ramana Ashram: A paved, stepped path begins directly behind Ramana Ashram. This path leads straight up to Skandasramam first. It takes about 30-40 minutes of moderate climbing. From Skandasramam, a downhill path leads to Virupaksha Cave (about 15 minutes away).
  2. From the Arunachaleswarar Temple side: A path begins behind the main temple. This path leads first to Virupaksha Cave, and from there you can climb higher to Skandasramam.

Essential Trekking Tips

  • Timings: The caves are open from 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM. Start your trek early in the morning (around 7:30 AM) to avoid the scorching sun.
  • Footwear: While many devotees climb barefoot as a sign of respect to the holy hill, wearing sturdy walking shoes is completely fine and recommended if you are not used to walking barefoot on rough stones.
  • Water: Carry a water bottle. The climb will make you sweat, and staying hydrated is critical.
  • Monkeys: The hill is home to many monkeys. Do not carry food openly in your hands or visible plastic bags, as they might try to snatch it.

Rest and Recover

After a powerful morning meditating in the caves, return to a cool, quiet, and comfortable room to integrate your experience.
