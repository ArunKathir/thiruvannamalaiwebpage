What to Expect at Sri Ramana Ashram

A visitor's guide to Sri Ramanasramam, including daily schedules, dining, meditation halls, and etiquette.

Sri Ramanasramam (Ramana Ashram) is the spiritual home of Bhagavan Sri Ramana Maharshi, one of India's most revered sages. Located at the foothills of the sacred Arunachala hill in Tiruvannamalai, the ashram is a sanctuary of peace, attracting truth-seekers from across the globe.

The Ashram Atmosphere

Unlike commercialized ashrams, Sri Ramanasramam maintains an incredibly silent, austere, and authentic atmosphere. The focus here is entirely on self-inquiry ("Who am I?") and silent meditation. There are no mandatory courses, fees for meditation, or rigid initiation rituals. Visitors are free to sit in the meditation halls, walk the grounds, or climb the path to Skandashram on the hill.

Key Places Inside the Ashram

  • The Old Hall: This is where Sri Ramana Maharshi spent most of his time from 1922 to 1950. It is a deeply powerful space for silent meditation.
  • Sri Maharshi's Samadhi: The shrine built over the tomb of Sri Ramana Maharshi. It is the central focal point of the ashram where daily pujas are performed.
  • Matrubhuteswara Temple: The temple built over the samadhi of Ramana Maharshi's mother. Beautiful Vedic chanting takes place here every morning and evening.
  • The Library: Houses an extensive collection of spiritual books in multiple languages.

Ashram Etiquette and Rules

  • Silence: Maintain strict silence in the Old Hall and around the Samadhi shrines.
  • Dress Code: Modest clothing is strictly enforced. Shoulders and knees must be covered. No shorts, sleeveless tops, or revealing clothing for both men and women.
  • Photography: Photography is not permitted inside the meditation halls or the Samadhi shrine.
  • Footwear: Footwear must be removed at the entrance of the ashram and left at the designated shoe racks.

Where to Stay Near Ramana Ashram?

Staying near Sri Ramanasramam is highly recommended for spiritual seekers who want to visit the ashram daily for early morning chanting or evening meditation. The area around the ashram is peaceful and filled with cafes, organic stores, and bookstores.
