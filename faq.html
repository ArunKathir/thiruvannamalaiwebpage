Arunachaleswarar Temple

The temple is generally open from 5:30 AM to 12:30 PM, and from 3:30 PM to 9:30 PM. However, during major festivals like Karthigai Deepam or full moon days (Pournami), the timings may be extended to accommodate the crowds.
Yes, traditional attire is required. Men should wear a dhoti or formal pants with a shirt (shorts and lungis are not allowed). Women should wear a saree, half-saree, or a modest churidar/salwar kameez. Avoid sleeveless tops and tight clothing.
On normal weekdays, darshan can take 30 minutes to 1 hour. On weekends or minor festival days, it might take 2-3 hours. During Pournami (Full Moon) and Karthigai Deepam, wait times can extend to 5-10 hours due to the immense crowds. Special darshan tickets can sometimes reduce wait times.
It is one of the Pancha Bhoota Stalas, representing the element of Fire (Agni). It is believed that Lord Shiva manifested here as a massive, infinite column of fire to settle a dispute between Brahma and Vishnu. Devotees believe that merely thinking of Arunachala grants liberation (Mukthi).
Foreigners and non-Hindus are allowed into the temple complex and can explore the massive courtyards, beautiful architecture, and outer shrines. However, entry into the innermost sanctum (Garbhagriha) where the main Lingam resides is traditionally restricted to Hindus.

Girivalam Guide

Girivalam (Giri meaning hill, and Valam meaning coming around) is the sacred practice of circumambulating the Arunachala Hill. It is believed that walking barefoot around the hill destroys karma, fulfills wishes, and accelerates spiritual liberation.
The Girivalam path is exactly 14 kilometers long. It generally takes 3 to 4 hours at a normal walking pace. If you stop at various shrines, ashrams, and lingams along the way, it may take 4 to 6 hours.
The most auspicious time is during Pournami (Full Moon). However, millions gather on this day. If you prefer a peaceful, meditative walk, you can do Girivalam on any normal day. Early mornings (4 AM to 7 AM) or late evenings after 5 PM are best to avoid the heat.
Yes. You should walk barefoot. Walk on the left side of the road facing the hill. Maintain silence, chant 'Om Namah Shivaya' or 'Arunachala Shiva', and avoid worldly conversations. Do not smoke, drink alcohol, or eat non-vegetarian food before the walk.
There are eight major Lingams (Ashta Lingams) naturally positioned along the Girivalam path, each associated with a specific direction and astrological benefit: Indra Lingam (East), Agni Lingam (South-East), Yama Lingam (South), Niruthi Lingam (South-West), Varuna Lingam (West), Vayu Lingam (North-West), Kubera Lingam (North), and Isanya Lingam (North-East). Devotees stop to worship at each one.

Ramana Ashram & Spirituality

Sri Ramana Maharshi was one of India's greatest modern sages. He arrived in Tiruvannamalai in 1896 as a teenager and never left the mountain. He taught the path of Advaita Vedanta (non-dualism) and Self-Inquiry, encapsulated in the simple but profound question "Who am I?", guiding seekers toward self-realization.
The ashram is generally open to visitors from 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM and from 2:00 PM to 6:30 PM. The Samadhi hall and Old Hall are open for quiet meditation.
Both caves are located on the slopes of Arunachala Hill. The trek starts from a paved path behind Ramana Ashram. Virupaksha cave (where Ramana Maharshi lived for 17 years) is about a 20-30 minute hike, and Skandashram is another 15 minutes further up the hill. The path is paved but steep.
The ashram does offer limited accommodation for sincere devotees. It must be booked months in advance via their official website and is subject to strict approval. For short-notice stays, or if you prefer more amenities, it is best to book a room near the ashram through our service.
The Old Hall at Ramana Ashram, Virupaksha Cave, Skandashram Cave, the inner corridors of the Arunachaleswarar Temple, and the nearby Seshadri Swamigal Ashram are all considered deeply powerful places for meditation.

Travel, Logistics & Local Info

From Chennai (190 km), you can take a bus from Kilambakkam (KCBT) or a direct train, taking about 4-5 hours. From Bangalore (200 km), frequent buses are available from Hosur/Majestic, or you can take a taxi taking about 4-5 hours. The nearest major airport is Chennai (MAA).
The best time to visit is during the winter months, from November to February, when the weather is cool and pleasant. Summer (March to June) can be extremely hot, with temperatures exceeding 40°C, making daytime activities and barefoot Girivalam very difficult.
As of now, Ola and Uber do not operate reliably in Tiruvannamalai. You will need to rely on local auto-rickshaws (always negotiate the fare before getting in, or ask for a share-auto). Food delivery is available via Swiggy and Zomato, but options may be limited compared to big cities.
Yes, it is generally very safe. Thousands of solo female travelers and foreign nationals visit every year. However, standard safety precautions apply: avoid walking alone in isolated areas (including the Girivalam path) late at night, and dress modestly to respect local culture.
Tiruvannamalai is a haven for vegetarian food. You will find traditional South Indian meals (thalis) and tiffin centers near the main temple. Additionally, there are many organic, vegan, and sattvic cafes scattered around the Ramana Ashram area catering specifically to international spiritual seekers.
