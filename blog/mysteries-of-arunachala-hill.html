The Mysteries of Arunachala

Why a singular mountain in South India has drawn seekers and sages for millennia.

In Tiruvannamalai, the mountain is not just a backdrop to the town; the mountain is the town. Arunachala is not considered the abode of Shiva (like Mount Kailash in the Himalayas); it is considered to be Shiva himself in physical form.

For thousands of years, this solitary hill rising from the plains of Tamil Nadu has radiated a magnetic energy, pulling in saints, yogis, and modern-day seekers. What makes Arunachala so mysteriously powerful?

1. The Myth of the Infinite Pillar of Fire

According to the Puranas (ancient Hindu texts), there was once an argument between Lord Brahma (the creator) and Lord Vishnu (the preserver) over who was supreme. To settle the dispute, Lord Shiva appeared before them as a massive, infinite column of fire that pierced the heavens and the earth.

He challenged them to find the end of the pillar. Brahma flew upwards as a swan to find the top, while Vishnu dug downwards as a boar to find the base. Both failed, humbling their egos before the infinite nature of Shiva.

Upon their request, Shiva condensed this blinding, infinite fire into the solid form of a hill so that humans could gaze upon him and worship. That hill is Arunachala. (Aruna = red/fire, Achala = immovable mountain).

2. The Geological Mystery

Fascinatingly, modern geology lends a unique perspective to the ancient myths. Geologists have classified the rock of Arunachala as Charnockite, an incredibly ancient form of rock. Studies suggest that Arunachala is part of the oldest rock formations on the entire planet, predating the Himalayas by billions of years. It has literally stood the test of time.

3. Sri Ramana Maharshi and the Magnetic Pull

Perhaps the most famous modern testament to Arunachala's power is the story of Sri Ramana Maharshi. In 1896, as a 16-year-old boy in Madurai, he heard the word "Arunachala." He described it as a magnetic pull that consumed him entirely.

He secretly left his home, took a train to Tiruvannamalai, and never left the mountain for the rest of his life (over 50 years). He always stated that it was the mountain itself that was his Guru, and that Arunachala radiated the silent teaching of "Who am I?".

4. The Power of Girivalam

Because the mountain is considered the physical body of the divine, the primary form of worship is circumambulation (walking around it), known as Girivalam. Sages say that walking barefoot around the 14-kilometer perimeter on a Full Moon (Pournami) physically burns karma and stills the mind.
