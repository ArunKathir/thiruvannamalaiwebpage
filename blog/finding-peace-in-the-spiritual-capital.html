Finding Inner Peace

Reflections on the transformative atmosphere of Tiruvannamalai.

There is a saying among regular visitors to Tiruvannamalai: "You don't choose to come to Arunachala; Arunachala calls you."

The Contrast of Chaos and Quiet

When you first arrive, the chaos of the bus stand and the main roads might overwhelm you. It looks like any other busy Indian town. But the moment you step into the Ramana Ashram, or take your first steps on the Girivalam path, or simply look up at the mountain, a profound silence descends upon your mind.

The Lingering Presence

The peace found here is not the peace of a quiet forest; it is a dynamic, charged peace. It is the peace of a thousand years of continuous prayer, meditation, and devotion. The saints who lived here—the Nayanmars, Ramana Maharshi, Seshadri Swamigal—left behind a spiritual residue that you can physically feel in the air.

Letting Go

To truly find peace in the spiritual capital, you must let go of your itinerary. Do not treat Tiruvannamalai as a checklist of temples to visit. Instead, sit by a temple tank. Watch the peacocks at the ashram. Drink a chai watching the sun set behind the holy hill. When you stop striving, the connection with God happens automatically.
