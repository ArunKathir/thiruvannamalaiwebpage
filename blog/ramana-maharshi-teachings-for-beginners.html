Ramana Maharshi for Beginners

An introduction to the profound simplicity of Self-Inquiry and the question: "Who am I?"

If you visit Tiruvannamalai, you will inevitably hear about Sri Ramana Maharshi. He is considered one of the greatest sages of the 20th century. However, unlike many gurus, he did not demand blind faith, complex rituals, or difficult yoga postures. His teaching was radically simple and direct.

Here is a beginner-friendly overview of his core philosophy.

1. The Illusion of the "I" (The Ego)

Ramana Maharshi taught that the root cause of all human suffering—fear, anxiety, sadness, and desire—is the false belief that we are our body and our mind. We constantly say "I am hungry," "I am sad," or "I am a doctor." But who is this "I"?

According to the Maharshi, this "I-thought" (the ego) is a phantom. It only exists because we don't investigate it. Once we shine the light of awareness on it, it disappears.

2. The Practice: Self-Inquiry (Nan Yar?)

His primary method for awakening is called Self-Inquiry, or asking the question, "Who am I?" (Nan Yar?).

This is not an intellectual exercise to find a verbal answer. When a thought arises (e.g., "I am worried about tomorrow"), you catch it and ask, "To whom does this thought arise?" The answer is "To me." Then you ask the final question: "Who am I?" or "Where does this 'I' come from?"

By constantly tracing the "I" back to its source, the mind eventually quiets down and dissolves into the true Self (pure awareness).

3. Silence as the Ultimate Teaching

While Ramana Maharshi answered thousands of questions during his life, he always maintained that his true, most powerful teaching was transmitted in Silence.

Seekers would come from across the world with lists of complex philosophical questions. They would sit in the Old Hall in front of him, look into his eyes, and find that their minds had gone completely blank. The questions dissolved in his silent presence.

4. You Don't Have to Leave the World

A common misconception about spirituality is that you must abandon your family, quit your job, and live in a cave. Ramana Maharshi actively discouraged people from renouncing their lives.

He taught that if you go to a forest, your mind goes with you. The true renunciation is internal—renouncing the ego. You can practice Self-Inquiry while working at a desk, cooking dinner, or raising children.
