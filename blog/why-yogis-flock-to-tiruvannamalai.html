Why Yogis Flock Here

The spiritual magnetism of South India's holiest town.

If you walk down the streets of Tiruvannamalai, especially around the Ramana Ashram or the Girivalam path, you will see a fascinating mix of people: dreadlocked sadhus wrapped in orange robes, Western seekers sitting quietly in cafes, and dedicated yogis practicing in the shade.

The Spiritual Vortex

Why do they all come here? The answer lies in the mountain. Mount Arunachala is considered a spiritual vortex—a place where the veil between the physical world and the divine is incredibly thin. Yogis claim that meditating here yields results ten times faster than anywhere else in the world.

The Lineage of Masters

Tiruvannamalai has a continuous, unbroken lineage of enlightened masters. From the 63 Nayanmars of antiquity to modern giants like Sri Ramana Maharshi, Seshadri Swamigal, and Yogi Ramsuratkumar. Yogis flock here to sit in the aura of these great masters, which is said to still permeate the ashrams and caves.

A Lifestyle of Renunciation

The town supports a lifestyle of spiritual seeking. Free food (Annadanam) is distributed daily, and cheap accommodation is plentiful. This allows yogis to completely renounce worldly pursuits and focus entirely on connecting with God.
